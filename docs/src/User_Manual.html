1. What the device does

  • Counts only your word. You choose the word or mantra (for example Radha) and teach it by saying it about 30 times. From then on only that word is counted, also when chanted continuously without pauses. Talking, taps, claps, the TV and room noise are ignored. Until the word is taught, nothing is counted.
  • Knows who is chanting. Made for 2 people (for example a couple). Each trains their voice once; every jap is counted for whoever said it, and the screen switches to that person's name automatically.
  • Celebrates milestones. At every 100 japs of a person (100, 200 … 900) rings burst on the screen with the number; at every 1000 the screen flashes the word (RADHA) in colours with fireworks.
  • Shows everything on the screen: the name, that person's count for today, their grand total, malas (108 japs), the family totals, and the date and time in IST.
  • Closes the day at 12 midnight IST. Today's counts are added to each person's grand total, saved to history, and today starts again from 0.
  • Never loses counts. Counts are stored in the device's flash memory and survive power cuts. A micro-SD card, if inserted, keeps a backup log.
  • Web dashboard. From any phone or computer on the same WiFi you can see live counts, add people, train voices, correct counts, change settings, and download history.

3. Installing the firmware

  1. Install Visual Studio Code and its PlatformIO IDE extension.
  2. Open the project folder (radha) in VS Code. PlatformIO downloads everything it needs the first time (this can take several minutes).
  3. Choose your board in the PlatformIO toolbar at the bottom of VS Code (the "env" selector): env:esp32dev for the ESP32 DevKit V1, or env:esp32-s3-n16r8 for the ESP32-S3.
  4. Connect the board by USB. On the ESP32-S3 use the USB-C port marked UART (or COM).
  5. Click ✓ Build, then → Upload.
  6. Optional: click the plug icon (Serial Monitor) to see the device's log at 115200 baud.
If upload fails with "No serial data received", hold the BOOT button, press and release RST, then release BOOT and upload again.

4. First-time WiFi setup

  1. Power the device. After the welcome screen it shows WIFI SETUP with a hotspot name (for example JapCounter-A1B2) and an 8-digit password.
  2. On your phone open WiFi settings and join that hotspot, using the password shown on the screen.
  3. A setup page opens by itself. If it does not, open a browser and go to 192.168.4.1.
  4. Tap your home WiFi in the list, type its password and press Save & connect.
  5. The device restarts, joins your WiFi and shows Connected with its IP address. The time is set automatically within a few seconds.
WiFi setup screen
Counting works even without WiFi. You can tap Skip – use the counter without WiFi on the setup page. Without internet the clock is not set, so the day will not close at midnight until WiFi is available.

If your WiFi is down when the device starts, it opens the setup hotspot again, keeps counting, and retries your WiFi every 30 seconds. When your WiFi returns it reconnects and the hotspot closes by itself.

6. Teaching the word and the voices

Open the dashboard (http://japcounter.local or the IP address shown on the screen at start-up) or the Android app. Your phone must be on the same WiFi. One training run teaches the counter both the word and that person's voice.

Choose the word

  1. In Word counting (web) or Settings → Word (app), type the word or mantra in English letters, for example Radha, and save. The name is a label for the screen; the counter learns the actual sound from the training below.

Add a person

  1. In the People card type a name in English letters (up to 16 characters) and press Add person. Up to 2 people can be added.

Train their voice

  1. Press Erase voice & retrain under the person's name and confirm.
  2. The screen shows the name in yellow with a counter such as ARJUN 0/20.
  3. Only that person says the word, one at a time with a short pause (about half a second) after each, at their usual distance and volume. The pauses let the counter cut out clean examples. Each one is also counted for them.
  4. After 30 examples training stops by itself. The card shows voice trained (30 chants) · word: 20 patterns: the counter keeps the 20 most typical examples of the word for that person.
  5. Repeat for each person. From now on the web page says Counting only "Radha".
For the best results: train in the room where the device will be used, at normal volume, and keep the room quiet while training (taps and noises are rejected, but talking during training is not). Train again if a person's voice changes, e.g. after a cold, or if their chanting is often missed.
ButtonWhat it does
Erase voice & retrainErases the person's voice model and word patterns, then records new examples straight away. Counts are kept.
Add chantsAdds more chants to the existing voice (up to 40 are kept)
Teach fast chantingAfter normal training: the person chants fast and continuously for about 10 seconds. The counter cuts out 20 fast words and learns them (8 fast + 12 slow patterns are kept), so fast chanting is counted correctly.

RenameChanges the name; counts and voice are kept
RemoveDeletes the person, their counts and voice
Stop trainingEnds training early (at least 5 chants are needed to recognise a person)
Each person who chants should be trained. Someone who has not trained may still be counted, credited to the trained person whose voice is closest.

7. Daily use

  • Switch the device on and chant. Every "Radha" is counted for whoever is chanting, with or without pauses — "radharadharadha…" works. The name and count appear on the screen.
  • Talking, coughing, tapping the device or the TV are not counted.
  • Up to about 3 words per second are counted. If you usually chant fast, use Teach fast chanting once after training (section 6).
  • Press the BOOT button once to pause while talking to someone; press again to resume.
  • One person at a time gives the best results. If two people chant together, the jap is counted once, for one of them.

9. Correcting counts

Each person (and Guest) has +1, −1 and Set today… buttons on the dashboard. For example, if a jap was given to the wrong person, press −1 on the wrong person and +1 on the right one.

11. Midnight, history and the SD card

  • At 12:00 midnight IST each person's count for the day is added to their grand total and written to history; "Prev day" then shows that day's count.
  • If the device was off at midnight, the day is closed as soon as it gets the time again.
  • History is kept in the device (download it as CSV from the dashboard). With an SD card inserted, every day is also appended to japlog.csv on the card, and the names are in names.txt.
  • Reset all counts erases counts and history in the device for everyone (names and voices are kept). The SD card log is not erased.

13. How well voice recognition works

The device measures the tone and pitch pattern of each jap (its "voice fingerprint") and compares it with the trained chants of every person.

SituationExpected result
Clearly different voices (man / woman / child), one at a timeGood — most japs get the right name
Similar voices (e.g. two brothers)Often mixed up; train with 30–40 chants and raise Strictness
Two or more people chanting togetherCannot be separated; counted once for one person
Whispering, a cold, very different distanceLess accurate; use Add chants to include these conditions
Recognition is a helper, not a guarantee. Check the counts now and then and correct them with the +1 / −1 buttons if needed.

14. Troubleshooting

ProblemWhat to do
Screen stays white or blackCheck the TFT wiring (CS, A0, RESET, SDA, SCK). LED must go to the backlight pin (GPIO 4 on DevKit V1, GPIO 7 on S3). Check that the PlatformIO build target matches your board.
Wrong colours or a strip of garbage at the edgeIn include/config.h change TFT_TAB to INITR_GREENTAB or INITR_REDTAB and upload again.
Screen shows NO MIC SIGNAL (red bar), or the dashboard says "Microphone not working"The counter already tried both mic channels at start-up and got nothing, so it is a wiring fault. Check SD, SCK and WS against the wiring table, VDD on 3V3 (not 5V), GND, and L/R on GND, then press EN. The serial log line starting with [mic] says exactly what was found.
Nothing is counted, screen says TEACH WORDThe word has not been taught yet. Train at least one person (section 6).
Nothing is counted (word taught)Is it paused? Chant a little louder or closer to the microphone; lower Word matching strictness.
Some chants are missedLower Word matching strictness by 1–2, or retrain that person, chanting the examples at your normal speed.
Other sounds are countedRaise Word matching strictness by 1–2. Make sure only trained people chant near the counter.
Training does not advance (stays 0/20)Say the word louder, with a clear half-second pause after each one; taps and whispers are not accepted as examples.
Lots of background noiseKeep the counter off tables with fans or motors on them (a soft pad under it helps). Use a short separate 3V3 wire to the mic and add a 10 µF capacitor across its VDD and GND.
Wrong namesRetrain the people involved with more chants; raise Strictness.
Everything goes to GuestLower Strictness, or train again in the room where the device is used.
Time shows --:--:--No internet. Counting continues; the clock is set once WiFi works.
Cannot open japcounter.localUse the IP address shown on the screen at start-up, or look for "JapCounter" in your router's device list.
Forgot / changed WiFi passwordHold BOOT for 5 seconds, then do the WiFi setup again.
No "SD" in the top barFormat the card as FAT32, check the SD_CS and SD_MISO wires against the wiring table for your board, restart.

16. The Android app

The Naam Jap app (NaamJap-1.1.0.apk) controls the counter from an Android phone (Android 8.0 or newer) on the same WiFi.

Installing

  1. Copy NaamJap-1.1.0.apk to the phone (USB cable, Google Drive, WhatsApp to yourself…).
  2. Open it. Android asks to allow installing apps from this source: allow it for the app you opened it with, then tap Install.
  3. If Play Protect warns that the app is unknown, choose Install anyway — the app is not from the Play Store, but it is signed and talks only to your counter.

Connecting

  1. Make sure the counter's WiFi is set up (section 4) and the phone is on the same WiFi.
  2. Open the app. It searches the WiFi and lists the counter by name, e.g. JapCounter-A1B2. Tap it.
  3. If nothing is found (some routers block discovery), type the counter's IP address under Connect by IP address.
  4. The app remembers the counter and connects to it automatically next time. If the router gives the counter a new address, the app finds it again by itself.
New counter without WiFi? On the app's first screen, follow New counter?: join the counter's hotspot in the phone's WiFi settings, then tap Open setup page to choose your home WiFi.
